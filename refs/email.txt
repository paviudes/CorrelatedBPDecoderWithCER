    Thanks for clarifying the standard definition, where twirling a state $\rho$ by a group $G$ entails computing the average state:
\rho_T = \sum_{g \in G} g \rho g^\dagger

If we compare three scenarios for a single QEC round under pure coherent physical error model: 
1. measuring the not recording the error syndrome (neither active nor passive QEC) : Twirling by the Stabilizer group
2. measuring the syndrome and recording the outcome but not applying active recovery (passive QEC)
3. measuring the syndrome and performing the recovery (active QEC)
we know the effective logical channel is non-unitary in (1), and remains unitary in (2) and (3).

The off-diagonal vs diagonal scaling in the process matrix of the logical channel (distance "d" code, and t = (d-1)/2) as a function of the physical rotation angle "theta", in each case is:
1. Diagonal: theta^(2 t) and off-diagonal: theta^t . I got this from Beale et al. https://arxiv.org/pdf/1805.08802.
2. Diagonal: (\pm theta)^(2 t) and off-diagonal: (\pm theta)^t. This is the pure unitary channel Robin pointed out. Here the "\pm" comes from the detail of whether the previous round's correction commutes with the rotation axis or not.
3. Diagonal: theta^(d) and off-diagonal: theta^(d/2). The exact form is also in (Eq 51) of Bravyi et al. https://arxiv.org/pdf/1710.02270. Pradnesh had also derived this process matrix.

If we perform "m" rounds of QEC and ask the question: how do the diagonal and offdiagonal elements of the logical channel (distance d code) behave with the physical rotation angle "theta" and "m"?
1. Diagonal: theta^(2 m t) and off-diagonal: \theta^(2 m t). So this approaches a decoherence map as m and d increases.
2. Diagonal: (\sqrt(m) theta^(2 t)) while off-diagonal: (\sqrt(m) \theta^t). So this too has a accumulation characteristic of a twirled map. Althought this is not twirling per the definitions. The physics of the effective noise process is unitary.
3. Diagonal: \theta^(2 t m) and off-diagonal: \theta^(t m). This has the signature of a coherent map.
